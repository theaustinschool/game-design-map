## Conflicts noticed

- Atomicity on input vs as outcome. The introduction's "each Zettel only contains one unit of knowledge" (quoted at /posts/principle-of-atomicity-difference-between-principle-and-implementation/) vs "don't push for atomicity right away" (/posts/how-to-research-the-archive/) and "a note can totally start as a dumping ground" (Nori, /posts/on-developing-deep-knowledge-work-practice/).
- Note length. "A base note is constrained to about three sentences" (Allen Wilson, /posts/playing-zettelkasten-rpg-through-arbitrary-constraints/) and concise mythologem notes vs "Write mini-essays" (/posts/stop-merely-pointing-at-ideas/) and few-statement notes as a failure mode (/posts/strategic-gambling-valuable-ideas/, /posts/principle-of-atomicity-difference-between-principle-and-implementation/).
- Literature notes and excerpts. "Death to literature notes!" (Sascha, /posts/field-report-6-phd-student-3-years/) and Luhmann "not: excerpts!" (/posts/luhmanns-zettel-translated/) vs writing an excerpt first and processing it later (/posts/field-report-9-excerpt-process/) and keeping excerpts in a feeder system (/posts/building-a-second-brain-and-zettelkasten/); Ahrens' literature notes as reference-manager entries (/posts/concepts-sohnke-ahrens-explained/).
- Where source records live. Luhmann: bibliographical notes for each read source inside the Zettelkasten (/communications-with-zettelkastens/) vs sources kept in an outside feeder system and not kept at all after processing (/posts/open-letter-cal-newport/, /posts/field-report-9-excerpt-process/).
- Folgezettel. Lüdecke (quoted): Folgezettel provide context links don't, and you first decide which topic a thought fits (/posts/embrace-dark-folgezettel-power/, /folgezettel/) vs Sascha: links provide richer context, placement is arbitrary, and he rejects Folgezettel (/posts/embrace-dark-folgezettel-power/, /posts/the-friction-fallacy/). Scheper argues against fluid find-and-replace systems in favour of a fixed numeric-alpha tree (/posts/introduction-antinet-zettelkasten/) vs structure-note-first refactoring (/posts/the-friction-fallacy/).
- Revising notes. Modify notes in place, refactor, rewriting is thinking (/posts/supercharging-long-thinking/, /posts/rewriting-notes-is-thinking/, /posts/the-friction-fallacy/) vs caution about revising permanent notes, keeping the history of mistakes (/posts/concepts-sohnke-ahrens-explained/), and the Folgezettel practice of adding variation notes instead of editing (described in /posts/the-friction-fallacy/). /posts/about-thinking-notes/ offers both options.
- What may enter the Zettelkasten. Luhmann 9/8a2 "don't put just treated notes in" and 9/8i "all arbitrary ideas ... can be brought in" (/posts/luhmanns-zettel-translated/) vs only ideas above a relevance/value threshold (/posts/rewriting-notes-is-thinking/), not merely-interesting ideas (/posts/should-you-have-a-note-goal-per-day/), and no raw captures with an #inbox tag (Christian, /posts/stop-caring-about-your-inbox/).
- Audience. Notes may make sense "to you and only you" (/posts/rewriting-notes-is-thinking/) vs understandable to other people (/posts/athletic-training-zettelkasten-value/) and "Can you present the idea to another person" (/posts/get-what-you-want-from-highlighting/).
- Titles. Title states content and is separate from ID (/posts/the-hidden-problem-with-note-titles-as-links-and-how-to-fix-it/, /posts/re-backlinks-should-be-context-rich/) vs titles carrying metadata: author-year citations (Jeannel King, /posts/field-report-6-phd-student-3-years/), status symbols (Allen Wilson), "T" prose marker (/posts/zettelkasten-fiction-writing-part-4-create-story/), rewritten for search (/posts/field-report-4-what-i-learned-writing-thesis-with-zettelkasten/).
- Tags. Object tags only; topic tags are a mistake (/posts/embrace-dark-folgezettel-power/, /posts/the-friction-fallacy/) vs scholar-name tags for resonance (/posts/field-report-4-what-i-learned-writing-thesis-with-zettelkasten/) and ingredient tags (/posts/how-to-create-value-in-zettelkasten/).
- Backlinks. Sascha: backlinks lack consciously stated context and bloat (/posts/re-backlinks-should-be-context-rich/) vs Jared Gorski (quoted there): backlinks should be context-rich and are meaningful.
- Highlighting. Ahrens (via Lengyel): read with pen and paper and don't highlight or mark up books (/posts/concepts-sohnke-ahrens-explained/) vs highlight with a pencil and the Barbell Method (/posts/daily-deep-reading-practice/, /posts/should-you-have-a-note-goal-per-day/).
- Quotes. Luhmann: quotes only worth collecting when exceptionally concise (/communications-with-zettelkastens/) and quotes must be rewritten before entry (/posts/how-to-create-value-in-zettelkasten/) vs a quote collection has some value (same post) and an interconnected quote collection violates the spirit of a Zettelkasten (/posts/little-machines/).
- Analog vs digital. Scheper: analog is a principle and digital a compromise (/posts/introduction-antinet-zettelkasten/) vs "use paper to assist thinking and store everything digital" (Sascha, /posts/field-report-6-phd-student-3-years/).
- Effort per note. Every note should be a struggle or mini-essay (/posts/stop-merely-pointing-at-ideas/) vs not every note needs to be a stroke of genius (/posts/optimal-concentration-success-recipe/), and effort should match idea tier (/posts/should-you-have-a-note-goal-per-day/).
